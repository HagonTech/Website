Security

Trust starts with limits.

Hagon is designed so customers can see what they authorized, what Hagon did, what evidence supports a conclusion, and where the product deliberately stops.

Tenant isolation

Customer-owned records are tenant-scoped, with PostgreSQL row-level security used where applicable.

Passive before active

Passive Recon uses external information without requiring active-testing authority. Direct interaction with customer infrastructure is a separate capability and requires explicit authorization.

Explicit authorization

Domain control, asset attribution, public network scope, and active-testing authority are separate concepts. Discovery never grants permission to scan. Exact hostname authorization permits only bounded interaction with that logical service; it does not grant general authority over the provider IP that serves it.

Evidence boundaries

Recon observations, candidate exposures, and vulnerability matches remain separate from Findings and Score until Validation supports a conclusion.

Bounded validation

Hagon uses least-intrusive, non-destructive active testing and Validation with centrally enforced safety limits. Scanner engines are subordinate to Hagon authorization and safety controls. Standard operation does not include denial-of-service testing, brute force, persistence, destructive exploitation, or lateral movement.

Data minimization

Hagon collects the evidence needed to establish a condition, not unnecessary customer data. Raw scanner and website artifacts are bounded and minimized, while durable normalized security history is retained when it is needed to explain what happened.

Accountable history

Authorization, lifecycle, ownership, remediation, delivery, and Score histories are designed to preserve an explainable record.

Responsible disclosure

A public vulnerability-reporting channel and security contact will be published before general availability.
