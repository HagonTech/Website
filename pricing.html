Pricing principles

Price the scope. Not the problems.

Hagon pricing is being designed around the public infrastructure a customer explicitly authorizes for active assessment. Discovering more should not become a financial penalty.

01

Start with your primary domain

Passive recon begins with the organization's primary domain. Discovered subdomains and related external observations do not automatically increase billing.

02

Authorized public IP scope

The primary commercial direction is based on public IP space the customer explicitly authorizes for active assessment,not every address or hostname Hagon discovers.

03

Discovery does not increase your bill

New hostnames, services, certificates, technologies, provider relationships, and unknown assets found through recon remain useful visibility without becoming surprise line items.

04

No per-finding incentive

Hagon should never make more money simply because it found more problems. Findings, remediation guidance, revalidation, and history belong to the operating lifecycle.

Same evidence quality.

Smaller organizations should receive the same standard of recon, intelligence correlation, validation, evidence, remediation guidance, and revalidation as larger organizations.

Scope changes are explicit.

If expanding authorized infrastructure would move a customer into a different plan, Hagon should make that clear before the scope changes.

Private-beta pricing is still being validated.

Final commercial plans and public rates have not yet been published. Early access may be complimentary or arranged as a paid pilot.

Payment never grants testing permission.

Commercial access and active-assessment authorization remain separate. Paying for Hagon never automatically authorizes infrastructure interaction.
